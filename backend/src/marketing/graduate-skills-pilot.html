01The challenge

Nigeria produces over 500,000 graduates a year. Many hold degrees but lack the practical, sector-specific skills employers screen for: cyber security in banks and telecoms, HSE and mapping in oil & gas, and digital marketing across all three. The gap is not ambition; it is evidence of skill.

02The pilot
  • Who: 200 graduates, NYSC members or final-year students, selected with your Ministry (about 50 per course).
  • How: fully online and self-paced, so participants can train from any state, alongside NYSC or other commitments.
  • Standard: every lesson day ends with a quiz and an assignment, and the course ends with a marked capstone project.
  • Name: co-branded under a programme name your Ministry chooses.

04What your Ministry receives
  • Monthly progress reports per participant: lessons completed, quizzes, assignments, and anyone stalled for 7+ days, so support reaches them early.
  • A dated attendance record for each participant, week by week, as evidence of training delivered.
  • A certificate and a marked capstone project for every graduate who completes, showing work an employer can see.
  • A final pilot report with completion rates and outcomes, to decide whether to scale nationally.

The pilot builds employability. It does not guarantee jobs, and no participant will be promised one.

05Cost and timeline

At standard fees, 200 places are worth ₦32,500,000. The pilot fee, any sponsorship discount and the payment milestones are to be agreed with your Ministry.

  1. Month 1: agree terms, select participants, onboard them to the platform.
  2. Months 1–3: training, with monthly progress reports.
  3. Month 4: capstone marking, certificates and the final pilot report.
