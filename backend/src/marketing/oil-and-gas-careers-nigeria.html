Overview

Nigeria's oil and gas sector is still one of the country's largest employers of graduate talent, but very little of that hiring happens through open, direct-experience roles — almost all of it runs through structured graduate trainee programmes designed for people who've never worked in the industry before.

That means "no experience" isn't the obstacle it feels like. The real filter, at graduate-trainee stage, is whether you can show up with a specific, demonstrable skill rather than a general degree — and whether you understand the one policy that shapes almost every hiring decision in this sector: the Nigerian Content Act. This guide walks through both: where the jobs actually sit, what Nigerian Content compliance means for you as a candidate, and which skills consistently show up in graduate-trainee shortlists for 2026.

The landscape

Where oil and gas jobs in Nigeria actually are

Hiring in the sector runs across three broad tiers. International oil companies (IOCs) like Shell, Chevron, TotalEnergies, and ExxonMobil run some of the best-known graduate trainee programmes and typically hire the most competitively, with structured, multi-stage application cycles. Indigenous and service companies — the tier NCDMB's Nigerian Content rules were specifically designed to grow — hire in larger volume and often more consistently year to year. NNPC and its subsidiaries sit alongside both as the state-owned anchor of the sector, with their own recruitment cycles.

Geography matters too. Corporate, commercial, and head-office functions cluster in Lagos; operational, technical, and field-based roles cluster in Port Harcourt and the wider Niger Delta, closer to production and pipeline infrastructure. A graduate trainee programme will often rotate candidates through both over the training period, so it's worth applying with either base in mind rather than ruling one out.

The policy that shapes hiring

NCDMB, Nigerian Content, and why it matters for your job search

The Nigerian Content Development and Monitoring Board (NCDMB) administers the Nigerian Content Act, which requires operators and their contractors to give first consideration to Nigerian personnel, goods, and training over foreign alternatives. That single rule is why Nigerian content oil and gas jobs exist in the volume they do: every operator and major contractor has a standing obligation to develop and hire Nigerian talent, not just a preference for it.

NCDMB training and job placement schemes are a direct, recognised route into the sector as a result — particularly into indigenous and service companies working to meet their own Nigerian Content obligations. Practically, this means the smartest move for a job search isn't only applying to the household-name IOCs; it's building skills the whole ecosystem of Nigerian Content-compliant employers is actively required to hire for.

What gets you shortlisted

The skills employers actually screen for at graduate-trainee stage

Four skill areas come up repeatedly in graduate-trainee and entry-level hiring across the sector, including HSE (health, safety, and environment) jobs in Nigeria's oil and gas industry, which remain a high-volume, standing hiring need on every site and rig:

01

GIS and drone mapping

Used across exploration, pipeline right-of-way monitoring, and land and asset surveys. Oil and gas companies increasingly run GIS and drone mapping training programmes for staff directly, which makes it a skill you can bring in already built rather than learn on the job.

02

HSE fundamentals

Health, safety, and environment roles are a constant, high-volume hiring need — every site, rig, and pipeline requires HSE coverage, and a working grasp of HSE fundamentals strengthens almost any other graduate-trainee application in the sector. See our dedicated HSE careers and training guide for the full regulatory framework and how to break in.

03

Digital and cyber literacy

Field and process operations are increasingly instrumented and networked, and operators are correspondingly more alert to the digital and cyber risk that comes with it — a candidate who can speak to basic cybersecurity awareness stands out from one who can't.

04

Renewable energy and the energy transition

Even traditional oil and gas majors are now hiring and training staff for the energy transition, so a working understanding of renewable energy systems is becoming a genuine differentiator rather than a niche interest, both for graduates and for staff already inside the sector.

For the full breakdown of which skill maps to which kind of role, see the dedicated digital skills for oil & gas guide.

Common questions

Frequently asked

Q1

How do I get a job in the oil and gas industry in Nigeria without experience?

Most operators and service companies hire fresh graduates through structured graduate trainee programmes rather than direct-experience roles, so "no experience" is the normal starting point, not a disadvantage. What separates candidates at that stage is demonstrable skill — HSE fundamentals, GIS and geospatial literacy, evidence you can apply them to a real project — not just a transcript. NCDMB-linked training and placement schemes are also a recognised route in.

Q2

What is NCDMB and how does it affect oil and gas jobs in Nigeria?

NCDMB administers the Nigerian Content Act, which requires operators and contractors to give first consideration to Nigerian personnel, goods, and training. In practice, Nigerian Content compliance is a real hiring lever: companies actively need Nigerian graduates with the right technical skills to meet it.

Q3

Which skills do oil and gas companies in Nigeria look for in graduate trainees?

GIS and drone mapping, HSE fundamentals, digital and cyber literacy, and a working understanding of renewable energy and the energy transition come up repeatedly — see the breakdown above.

Q4

Are oil and gas jobs in Nigeria concentrated in Lagos or Port Harcourt?

Broadly, corporate and head-office functions cluster in Lagos, while operational and field-based roles cluster in Port Harcourt and the wider Niger Delta. Graduate trainee programmes often rotate candidates through both.
