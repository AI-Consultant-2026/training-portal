How to Build a Portfolio During NYSC

A skill you've learned is only half the story — a portfolio is the evidence. Here's how to build one during service, before job-hunting starts.

Overview

If you've already chosen a course or a skill to focus on during service, the next question is what you'll actually have to show for it by the time you're job-hunting. Learning a skill isn't the same as having evidence of it — a portfolio is that evidence.

This is the "Build" and "Prove" part of Paleon's Learn → Build → Prove → Get Ready framework, made concrete for the NYSC year specifically: what actually counts as a portfolio piece, where to put it, and how to build one without a paid job to draw on yet.

Key takeaways

  • A portfolio demonstrates applied skill, not paid work history — coursework capstones, CDS projects, and self-directed practice all count.
  • One or two genuinely well-documented projects beat five shallow ones.
  • A completed Paleon capstone project is designed to be a presentable portfolio piece on its own, not just an internal assessment.
  • Where you host it matters less than what's in it — a PDF, a personal site, or LinkedIn's Featured section are all reasonable starting points.
  • Your specific contribution needs to be describable in your own words — a project you can't explain clearly doesn't function as evidence.

What actually counts

What "portfolio" means when you don't have a job yet

Four real sources of portfolio material during NYSC, none of which require prior paid work experience:

01

Course capstone projects

A completed Paleon capstone — a security audit, a GIS map, a marketing campaign, a content calendar, an HSE plan, depending on the course — is built specifically to be presentable, not just graded.

02

CDS projects

If your Community Development Service work involves a relevant digital skill, it counts — as long as you can describe your specific contribution, not just that you were present.

03

Self-directed practice projects

A project you built on your own initiative — outside any course or CDS requirement — shows genuine interest, not just compliance.

04

PPA-related work, where relevant

If your Primary Place of Assignment involves any part of your chosen skill, document it — even a small, real application counts more than a purely theoretical exercise.

Portfolio project ideas

Project ideas by skill area

General project types you can build during service, without claiming any single one guarantees a hiring outcome:

Cyber Security

A basic security-awareness audit

Review a small organisation's (real or hypothetical, with permission if real) common security gaps and write up findings and recommendations.

GIS & Drone Mapping

A small map of a local area

Map a genuinely useful area — your PPA's neighbourhood, a CDS project site — with clear documentation of the tools and method used.

Digital Marketing / Social Media

A mock content calendar or campaign plan

Build a realistic month-long content or campaign plan for a real or plausible brand, with reasoning for each choice, not just a list of post ideas.

Renewable Energy / HSE

A system or site plan write-up

A basic solar system sizing exercise, or a site HSE management plan for a real or hypothetical location, documented clearly enough that someone else could follow it.

Before you start

Common mistakes when building a portfolio

  • Waiting until you feel "ready" to start documenting — document as you go; reconstructing a project's details months later is harder and less convincing.
  • Building five shallow projects instead of one or two thorough ones — depth and clear explanation matter more than volume.
  • Listing a project without explaining your specific contribution — "I was part of this" is weaker than "I did this specific thing."
  • Overcomplicating the hosting decision — a free PDF or LinkedIn Featured post is a perfectly good starting point; don't delay on platform choice.
  • Assuming a portfolio alone guarantees an interview or job — it's evidence that strengthens an application, not a guarantee of any specific outcome.

Common questions

Frequently asked

Q1

What counts as a "portfolio" if I don't have a job yet?

Coursework capstones, self-directed projects, CDS-related work, and documented practice projects all count — a portfolio demonstrates applied skill, not paid work history.

Q2

Where should I host my portfolio?

A simple, free option — a PDF, a personal site, LinkedIn's Featured section, or a project-specific platform relevant to your skill — is enough to start. The content matters more than the platform.

Q3

How many projects do I need?

Quality over quantity — one or two genuinely well-documented projects beat five shallow ones.

Q4

Can my CDS project be part of my portfolio?

Yes, if it demonstrates a relevant digital skill and you can describe your specific contribution clearly.

Q5

Does Paleon's capstone project count as a portfolio piece automatically?

Yes — a completed capstone from any of Paleon's six courses is designed to be a genuine, presentable portfolio piece, not just an internal assessment.
