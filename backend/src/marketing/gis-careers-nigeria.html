Overview

GIS (Geographic Information Systems) is the practice of collecting, managing, and analysing spatial data to support real decisions — site selection, environmental monitoring, infrastructure planning. In Nigeria, GIS shows up across agriculture, urban planning, telecom, and oil & gas specifically, not any one sector exclusively.

This guide covers what a GIS career actually involves day to day, the tools you'll use, and how to get started. For the oil & gas application specifically, see the digital skills for oil & gas guide and the broader career hub.

Key takeaways

  • GIS work centres on collecting, cleaning, and analysing spatial data to support decisions, not just making maps.
  • GIS is used across agriculture, urban planning, telecom, and infrastructure in Nigeria — oil & gas is one significant application, not the only one.
  • QGIS (free, open-source) is a reasonable, low-cost tool to start with; ArcGIS (commercial) is also widely used by employers.
  • Many entry-level and support GIS roles don't strictly require a geography or surveying degree.
  • Drone mapping is a closely related but distinct skill — one method of collecting the data GIS software then analyses.

Day to day

What a GIS analyst actually does

The specific mix varies by employer and sector, but GIS work typically involves collecting and cleaning spatial data, building and maintaining maps, and producing analysis that feeds into real decisions — where to site something, what to monitor, how to plan infrastructure. It's applied, decision-support work, not an abstract mapping exercise.

Entry pathways

Do you need a geography degree?

Not necessarily. A geography or surveying background helps for certain technical and engineering-adjacent roles, but many entry-level and support GIS roles are open to graduates from other backgrounds — and, for certain roles, non-graduates — who can demonstrate practical tool skill and a portfolio. This varies by employer, so check individual job descriptions rather than assume a degree requirement either way.

Before you start

Common mistakes

  • Assuming GIS is only relevant to oil & gas — agriculture, telecom, urban planning, and infrastructure all use it too.
  • Starting with ArcGIS before QGIS fundamentals — the free, open-source route is a reasonable place to build foundational skill first.
  • Confusing GIS and drone mapping as the same skill — they're closely related but distinct; drone mapping is one data-collection method GIS then analyses.
  • Assuming a geography degree is always required — many entry-level and support roles are open on demonstrable skill alone.
  • Learning the software without building a real project — a portfolio piece (a genuine map of a real or plausible area) matters more than software familiarity alone.

Common questions

Frequently asked

Q1

What does a GIS analyst actually do day to day?

Typically: collecting and cleaning spatial data, building and maintaining maps, and producing analysis that supports decisions — site selection, environmental monitoring, infrastructure planning. The exact mix depends on the employer and sector.

Q2

Do I need a geography or surveying degree to work in GIS?

It helps for certain technical and engineering-adjacent roles, but many entry-level and support GIS roles are open to graduates from other backgrounds who can demonstrate practical tool skill, such as QGIS, and a portfolio.

Q3

Is GIS only relevant to oil & gas?

No — agriculture, urban planning, telecom (network and tower siting), and infrastructure all use GIS. Oil & gas is one significant Nigerian application, not the only one.

Q4

What software should I learn first?

QGIS is a widely used, free, open-source starting point. Many employers also use ArcGIS, a commercial platform — starting with QGIS is a reasonable, low-cost way to build foundational skill.

Q5

How does GIS relate to drone mapping?

They're closely related but distinct — drone mapping is one method of collecting the spatial data that GIS software then analyzes. See the Drone Mapping Careers pillar for that side of the pairing.

Q6

Can a non-graduate build a GIS career in Nigeria?

Yes, for certain entry-level and technical-support roles, where demonstrable tool skill and a portfolio matter more than a specific degree — this varies by employer.
