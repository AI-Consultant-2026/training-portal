01The Signal

This isn't a hiring problem. It's a training pipeline problem.

Nigeria's fintech sector alone now drives roughly 22% of the country's GDP growth — and hiring is its number-one operational constraint, not demand4. The banks competing with fintechs for the same small pool of "digitally fluent" hires are bidding against a shortage that a structured training pipeline can actually solve.

70% of employers report real difficulty finding qualified digital candidates, and 77% now treat digital skill as a critical hiring criterion rather than a bonus4. Nigeria has just 25,760 cybersecurity professionals for a population of over 220 million1. That gap doesn't close by widening the job posting — it closes by training the graduates already applying.

05The Mandate

Staff cybersecurity training isn't optional. CBN made it a requirement.

The Central Bank of Nigeria's Risk-Based Cybersecurity Framework for Deposit Money Banks and Payment Service Banks has been mandatory since 1 July 202410.

Its ten parts include explicit "awareness and training" and "personnel security" pillars: every employee, from junior staff to senior executives, must be trained on cyber hygiene, phishing recognition, and incident reporting, with periodic phishing exercises to test whether that training actually holds10. Banks must also complete an annual cybersecurity self-assessment (CSAT) and participate in NigFinCERT — non-compliance carries fines, and in serious cases, license consequences10.

Paleon holds no CBN endorsement and doesn't perform official CSAT assessments — we won't claim otherwise. What Paleon's Cyber Security Fundamentals course does is train staff at exactly the skill layer that framework requires: recognizing phishing, understanding social engineering, and knowing how to report an incident before it becomes a breach. It's the training input the compliance requirement already assumes someone is providing.
