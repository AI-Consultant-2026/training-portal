How to Write a CV With No Work Experience in Nigeria

No formal work history doesn't mean an empty CV. This guide covers what to lead with instead — honestly, without inventing an example CV and presenting it as a real one.

Last reviewed: 16 September 2026 · Reviewed by Paleon Training

Overview

No formal work history doesn't mean an empty CV. This guide covers what to lead with instead — honestly, without inventing an example CV and presenting it as a real one.

The structural fix is simple: reorder the standard CV sections so what you do have (projects, skills, coursework, NYSC) leads, and what you don't have (a long work history) isn't the first thing a recruiter notices missing.

Key takeaways

  • A CV with no work experience should reorder sections — lead with projects and skills, not a work-history section that's thin or empty.
  • A real capstone or portfolio project is one of the strongest things you can lead with in place of work history.
  • NYSC service, coursework, and volunteer or society roles are legitimate content — they just need to be framed around what you actually did, not just attended.
  • Generic objective statements and padded skill lists don't help a thin CV — specific project detail does.
  • See the main graduate CV guide for the full structure this one builds on.

What to lead with instead

Reordering the CV, not padding it

Start with a short profile line stating your field of interest and strongest demonstrable skill — not a generic "hardworking team player" objective. Follow it immediately with a projects/portfolio section: a real capstone project, freelance work, or coursework project, described by what you actually did and built, not just the course name.

Only after that should education and NYSC appear, framed around any real responsibility you held (coordinating something, leading a small initiative) rather than just dates attended. A skills section comes last — specific tools and skills you can actually demonstrate, not a long generic list.

Common questions

Frequently asked

Q1

Should I lie or exaggerate to fill a CV with no work experience?

No — never invent experience. The better fix is reordering what's genuinely there (projects, coursework, NYSC responsibilities) so it leads, rather than fabricating a work history.

Q2

What if I don't have a portfolio project yet?

A real capstone project from a structured course, or even a self-directed project you can describe honestly, is one of the strongest substitutes for work history — see the main graduate CV guide for a real phrasing example.

Q3

Does NYSC count as work experience?

It's not traditional employment, but genuine responsibilities you held during service (coordinating an initiative, leading a small project) are legitimate CV content when framed around what you actually did.

Q4

How long should a no-experience CV be?

One page is usually enough at this stage — a short, well-structured CV reads better than a padded one trying to look longer than it is.
