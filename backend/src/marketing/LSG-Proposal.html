Dear Mr Egube,

Paleon Digital Academy proposes a Lagos State Graduate Employability Campaign: 200 of the state's recent graduates, NYSC corps members and final-year students trained in practical, industry-focused digital skills. Every participant finishes with a real project, CV support and a reference, ready to compete for roles in the Banking, Telecoms and Oil & Gas companies headquartered across Lagos.

We are asking the State to sponsor the first cohort, co-brand the campaign and help us reach eligible graduates across all three senatorial districts: Lagos East, Lagos West and Lagos Central. In return, the State gets a measurable, publicly visible employability result within six months.

02Lagos State: a state that invests in its young people

Lagos is Nigeria's commercial centre and the home of its technology scene. The head offices of the country's major banks sit on Lagos Island, Victoria Island and Ikoyi. Telecoms operators and tech companies cluster in Ikeja, Yaba and Lekki, and oil and gas firms run their corporate offices from Victoria Island and Lekki. Institutions such as the University of Lagos in Akoka, Lagos State University in Ojo, Yaba College of Technology and LASUSTECH in Ikorodu produce thousands of graduates every year.

The Government's commitment to youth employment, technology and skills development gives this proposal its starting point. It offers the State a fast, low-risk way to put that commitment into practice:

  • Visible: a named, co-branded State programme with real participants and public success stories.
  • Fair: places spread across Lagos East, Lagos West and Lagos Central senatorial districts, reaching graduates from Badagry and Ojo to Ikorodu, Epe and Ibeju-Lekki, not only central Lagos.
  • Practical: delivered online on a phone, so participants in Alimosho, Agege or Ikorodu don't need to spend hours in traffic to attend.
  • Measurable: completion, portfolio, interview and job-placement figures reported back to the Government.
03About Paleon Digital Academy

Paleon Digital Academy Limited is a Nigerian digital skills academy. We train recent graduates, NYSC corps members and final-year students in the practical digital skills that employers in Nigeria's leading industries now expect.

Our model is Learn, Apply, Build:

  • Learn: short, structured daily lessons with video, reading and a quiz.
  • Apply: an assignment for every training day, based on a realistic workplace task.
  • Build: each course ends with a capstone project, so learners leave with evidence they can show an employer, not just a certificate.

The programme runs on our own learning portal at paleontraining.com. It is self-paced, works on a phone, gives lifetime access to course materials and tracks every learner's progress. Each graduate receives:

  • a certificate of completion;
  • an attendance record, usable as evidence for ITF and SIWES purposes;
  • CV support;
  • a reference for job applications, based on their completed coursework and capstone.

Anyone can try the first lesson of every course free, without an account, before committing.

04The challenge: degrees without job offers

Nationally, the National Bureau of Statistics' Labour Force Survey (Q2 2024) reported unemployment of 4.3%, youth unemployment of 6.5% and time-related underemployment of 9.2%. Those headline figures understate what graduates experience. Many are in casual or unrelated work while they wait for a role that uses their degree.

In Lagos the competition is sharper still, because graduates from across the country come to the city for the same jobs. The obstacles Lagos graduates face are consistent:

  • Every CV looks the same. Hundreds of applicants with similar degrees and grades apply for each graduate trainee role on Victoria Island or in Ikeja, so employers have no easy way to tell them apart.
  • There is no evidence of practical skill. Recruiters in banking, telecoms and oil and gas ask what a candidate can do. Most graduates have nothing to show beyond their transcript.
  • Digital skills are now assumed. Cyber security awareness, data and mapping tools, digital marketing and safety reporting are part of everyday work in these sectors, yet few degree programmes teach them in a practical way.
  • NYSC and final-year students run out of time. The service year and final year pass without a structured plan to become employable.
  • There are no references or networks. Graduates without industry contacts cannot get the referee or introduction that often decides who is shortlisted.
  • Good training is expensive and hard to reach. Quality private training is out of reach for most families, and attending classes in person can mean hours in Lagos traffic each day.

The result is talent that is not used. Graduates from Ikorodu, Badagry or Epe stay locked out of the jobs being created a few kilometres away.

07The proposal: Lagos State Graduate Employability Campaign

We propose that the Lagos State Government sponsors 200 Lagos graduates through the Paleon digital skills programme, under a co-branded campaign name such as "Lagos Digital Graduates 200".

Who is eligible

  • Recent graduates (within three years of graduation), NYSC corps members serving in Lagos State and final-year students.
  • Lagos State residents, or students of Lagos-based institutions.
  • Places shared fairly across Lagos East, Lagos West and Lagos Central, with targets for women and persons with disabilities, and outreach in Badagry, Ikorodu, Epe and Ibeju-Lekki as well as the city centre.

How participants are selected

  1. Application: open applications through a dedicated campaign page on paleontraining.com, promoted by the State and Paleon.
  2. Career Match and free lesson: each applicant completes the Career Match and the free first lesson of their chosen course. This shows commitment and stops places going to people who will not finish.
  3. Short screening: a short motivation statement and eligibility check.
  4. Final selection: a joint Paleon and State panel approves the 200 participants, about 50 per course, adjusted to demand.

What each participant receives

  • Full access to one Paleon course, with lifetime access to the materials.
  • Daily assignments, a capstone project and a certificate of completion.
  • An attendance record for ITF and SIWES purposes.
  • CV support: a CV rebuilt around their new skill and capstone project.
  • A reference for job applications in Banking, Telecoms and Oil & Gas, based on their verified coursework.
  • Live group sessions on Zoom for orientation, Q&A and interview preparation.

Value

At standard fees, 200 places (50 per course) are worth ₦32,500,000. For a State-sponsored cohort, Paleon proposes a 15% discount on the standard fee for every participant (to be agreed), to be paid in milestones tied to results (see Investment and reporting).

08Success stories and publicity

The campaign will be visible so that the Government gets credit for every graduate it helps.

  • Graduate spotlights: short video and photo stories of participants presenting their capstone projects, and later announcing interviews and job offers.
  • Paleon channels: our website, Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn and WhatsApp community, each post crediting the Lagos State Government.
  • Partner media: stories syndicated through associated outlets including AllAboutNigeria ONE Nigeria on Facebook, which reaches a wide Nigerian audience.
  • Launch and graduation events: a launch announcement and a graduation showcase in Ikeja or Victoria Island, where top capstone projects are presented to the Government and invited employers.
  • Press releases at launch, at mid-programme and at graduation, with the outcome figures.

All stories are published only with each participant's written consent, in line with the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023.

11Recommendations to make the campaign a success
  1. Start with a pilot, then scale. Treat the 200 as Cohort 1. If the targets are met, the State can expand to further cohorts (for example 1,000 graduates a year) with the evidence already in hand.
  2. Pay for results. Milestone payments tied to enrolment, completion and outcome reports protect public funds and make the programme easy to defend.
  3. Hold an employer roundtable. The Government convenes banks, fintechs, telcos and oil and gas firms on Victoria Island and in Ikeja to see the capstone showcase, and invites them to offer interviews, internships or placements to graduates.
  4. Co-brand the certificates. Certificates carry both the Lagos State Government and Paleon Digital Academy names, which adds weight with employers and credit for the State.
  5. Include everyone. Reserve places for women and persons with disabilities. Consider data bundles or device loans for participants in Badagry, Epe, Ikorodu and Ibeju-Lekki, where access is harder.
  6. Use the NYSC year. Work with the NYSC Lagos State Secretariat and SAED (Skills Acquisition and Entrepreneurship Development) so corps members serving in Lagos can use the programme during their service year.
  7. Work with Lagos institutions. Partner career offices at UNILAG, LASU, Yaba College of Technology and LASUSTECH to reach final-year students and to recognise the programme as career preparation.
  8. Back self-employment too. Digital Marketing and GIS graduates can freelance or start small businesses. Link interested graduates to the Lagos State Employment Trust Fund (LSETF) and other State enterprise programmes.
  9. Use independent, transparent selection. Published eligibility criteria and a joint selection panel give the programme credibility and guard against favouritism.
  10. Build an alumni network. Graduates join a Lagos Digital Graduates community and Paleon's ambassador programme, so they mentor the next cohort and the State keeps track of outcomes.

Paleon does not claim accreditation it does not hold. Where formal accreditation or alignment (for example with professional bodies) would strengthen the programme, we will pursue it with the State's support.
