How to List Your NYSC Service on Your CV

NYSC service is genuine experience if you frame it around what you actually did, not just the dates you served.

Last reviewed: 16 September 2026 · Reviewed by Paleon Training

Overview

NYSC service is genuine experience if you frame it around what you actually did, not just the dates you served.

The difference between a weak and a strong NYSC CV line is specificity about responsibility, not the service itself.

Key takeaways

  • List your NYSC Place of Primary Assignment like any other role — organisation, dates, and what you actually did.
  • Frame any real responsibility you held (coordinating a CDS project, a specific task at your PPA) specifically, not just "completed NYSC."
  • A CDS project you led or contributed to meaningfully is legitimate CV content, framed around your actual role in it.
  • Avoid listing NYSC as just a life-stage note ("currently serving") when it can instead demonstrate real, specific experience.
  • See how to turn a CDS project into a portfolio piece for the fuller version of this.

How to frame it

Like any other role, with real specifics

Treat your NYSC Place of Primary Assignment like any other work entry: organisation name, dates, and 1–2 specific lines about what you actually did there — not just "Completed National Youth Service Corps." If you held any real responsibility, even informal, name it specifically.

If you led or meaningfully contributed to a Community Development Service project, that's legitimate, specific CV content — framed around your actual role and what resulted, not just that the project happened. See how to turn a CDS project into a portfolio piece for developing this further.

Common questions

Frequently asked

Q1

Should I list my NYSC PPA as work experience on my CV?

Yes -- treat it like any other role, with the organisation, dates, and specific responsibilities, rather than just noting that you served.

Q2

Can a CDS project go on my CV?

Yes, if you led or meaningfully contributed to it -- frame it around your actual role and specific outcome, the same way you would any other project.

Q3

How do I make NYSC service sound like real experience, not just a formality?

Specificity about what you actually did, rather than a generic 'completed NYSC' line -- the same principle as any other CV entry.
