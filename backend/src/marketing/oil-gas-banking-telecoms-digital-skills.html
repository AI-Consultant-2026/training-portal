How to Stand Out When Applying to Oil & Gas, Banking and Telecoms Jobs in Nigeria

A single graduate-trainee intake at a major bank or telecom operator can attract tens of thousands of applications for a few hundred places — here’s what tends to matter in each of the three sectors, and how to adapt your CV.

Overview

Oil and gas, banking, and telecommunications remain three of the most competed-for sectors for Nigerian graduates and career changers, associated with stronger pay, structured graduate-trainee programmes, and long-term career paths — which is exactly why they are so hard to break into.

The mistake many candidates make is applying to all three sectors, and every employer within them, with the same CV, the same generic cover letter, and the same vague list of “skills.” Recruiters in these industries see this pattern constantly, and it rarely survives the first screening round. A necessary caveat before we go further: every employer, every role, and every job description within these sectors is different. What follows is a guide to common patterns, not a checklist that applies identically to every vacancy — always read the actual job description carefully.

Sector 1

Oil & Gas: What Tends to Matter

Nigeria’s oil and gas sector spans technical, operational, commercial, safety and support roles, and relevant skills vary considerably. Different roles have very different requirements — a field engineering role, an HSE officer role, a data analyst role and a corporate communications role at the same company will screen for almost entirely different things.

HSE

HSE awareness

Central to how the industry operates, given the physical risks involved. Specific safety-critical positions will usually require recognised certifications (such as NEBOSH) beyond any foundational course.

Cyber

Cybersecurity

As operations increasingly rely on digital systems and operational technology, basic awareness has become more relevant, particularly for roles touching IT, data or OT.

GIS

GIS & drone mapping

Used across exploration support, environmental monitoring, land and pipeline-related work, and infrastructure planning.

Energy

Renewable energy & digital systems

As companies diversify and invest in digital monitoring of energy assets, this is becoming more relevant for technically inclined candidates.

Marketing

Digital marketing

Relevant for commercial, communications and stakeholder-engagement functions — these are larger organisations than many candidates assume, with real marketing teams.

Data

Data & digital literacy

Comfort with data tools, reporting software and digital systems is increasingly assumed across administrative, technical and commercial support roles alike.

Sector 2

Banking & Financial Services: What Tends to Matter

Nigerian banks have become considerably more digital over the past decade, and this shows up clearly in what graduate-trainee and entry-level hiring tends to prioritise.

Cyber

Cybersecurity

With the growth of digital banking, mobile apps and online transactions, banks are increasingly attentive to fraud risk — relevant across many roles, not only IT.

Digital

Digital transformation awareness

Understanding how banks are shifting services online, and being comfortable discussing digital banking platforms, is a genuine advantage in interviews.

Marketing

Digital marketing

Banks run substantial digital marketing operations, from app-acquisition campaigns to product-specific promotions.

Social

Social media

Customer engagement increasingly happens through social channels — relevant to marketing, customer experience and communications functions.

Customer

Customer engagement

Much of retail banking hiring still prioritises strong communication and customer-facing skills alongside digital competence.

Risk

Risk & data awareness

A foundational understanding of risk concepts and comfort interpreting data is useful even outside explicitly analytical or compliance-adjacent roles.

Sector 3

Telecommunications: What Tends to Matter

Telecom operators in Nigeria combine large-scale technical infrastructure with consumer-facing digital products, creating a distinct mix of relevant skills.

Cyber

Cybersecurity

Telecom networks carry enormous volumes of customer data and are frequent targets for digital threats — relevant across technical and non-technical roles alike.

Marketing

Digital marketing & social

Telecom operators run large, continuous digital marketing operations around data bundles, airtime promotions and app-based services, with customer engagement substantially through social platforms.

GIS

GIS

Network planning, coverage mapping and infrastructure rollout rely on geospatial data and mapping tools.

Systems

Digital systems & technical awareness

General comfort with the platforms operators use for service delivery, and a working understanding of how telecom infrastructure functions, even for non-engineering roles.

Why the “Same CV for Every Employer” Approach Fails

Why it matters

Why should I customise my CV for each industry? Because a CV that reads as generic signals to a recruiter that you have not researched the role or employer, while a CV that reflects specific sector knowledge signals genuine interest and preparation — both of which matter when hundreds of other applicants are competing for the same seat.

Consider two applicants for a graduate-trainee analyst role at a Nigerian bank, with identical degree classifications. Applicant A submits a generic CV listing “strong communication skills” and “proficient in Microsoft Excel.” Applicant B submits a CV referencing a digital marketing project analysing campaign performance data, familiarity with digital banking trends, and basic data literacy through a described project. Applicant B has done nothing to guarantee an offer, but has given the recruiter concrete, sector-relevant reasons to take the application seriously.

The adaptation framework

How to Adapt Your CV for Each Industry

CV section What to adjust
Professional summary Rewrite it for each application to reference the specific sector and role type — avoid a single generic summary reused everywhere.
Skills section Prioritise the skills most relevant to that sector’s common demands, rather than listing every skill in no particular order.
Project evidence Lead with the project most relevant to the employer — a digital marketing campaign for a bank, a GIS project for oil and gas, a cybersecurity project for a telecom.
Relevant certifications List in order of relevance to the specific role, and briefly note what each covered if the name alone isn’t self-explanatory.
Internship/NYSC experience Reframe the same experience differently per employer — emphasise the parts of your PPA or internship that map onto that sector’s priorities.
Achievements Use specific, quantifiable detail where honestly possible, rather than vague claims.
Digital portfolio Link to it, and highlight the specific project most relevant to that employer at the top.
LinkedIn profile Keep it broad enough to represent you accurately, but make your headline and featured section highlight your current target sector.

Common questions

Frequently asked

Q1

What skills do oil and gas companies in Nigeria look for in entry-level candidates?

This varies significantly by role, but foundational HSE awareness, cybersecurity awareness, GIS and drone mapping familiarity, digital and data literacy, and (for commercial/communications roles) digital marketing skills are commonly relevant across different functions in the sector. Always confirm specific requirements against the individual job description.

Q2

Do I need a NEBOSH certificate to work in oil and gas HSE roles?

For dedicated HSE officer and safety-critical roles, employers typically expect recognised certifications such as NEBOSH. A foundational HSE course can build useful awareness and complement your profile, but it does not replace a recognised professional HSE certification where a specific role requires one.

Q3

What digital skills do Nigerian banks want from graduate trainees?

Commonly relevant areas include cybersecurity awareness, digital marketing, social media literacy, customer engagement skills, basic risk awareness, data literacy and general technology awareness, reflecting how digitally driven modern retail and commercial banking has become.

Q4

Is cybersecurity relevant if I'm not applying for an IT role in a bank or telecom company?

Yes, to a degree. Given how central digital security and fraud prevention are to both sectors, basic cybersecurity awareness is increasingly relevant even for non-IT, customer-facing and operational roles, not only dedicated technical positions.

Q5

Should I use the same CV for oil and gas, banking, and telecom applications?

It is generally more effective to adapt your CV's summary, prioritised skills, and highlighted project evidence for each sector, rather than sending an identical CV to all three. Recruiters can usually tell when an application has not been tailored to their specific role or sector.

Q6

How relevant is GIS to telecom jobs, given it's usually associated with mapping and land use?

GIS skills are relevant to telecom network planning and coverage mapping, since geospatial data underpins how operators plan infrastructure rollout and coverage analysis, making basic GIS familiarity a useful, if less obvious, skill for telecom-focused technical and field-support roles.

Q7

Can digital marketing skills help me get into oil and gas or banking, which seem like technical sectors?

Yes, both sectors run substantial marketing, communications and stakeholder-engagement functions, and digital marketing skills are relevant to those specific roles within otherwise technical or financial organisations, though not to every role in the sector.

Q8

What is the biggest mistake candidates make applying to these three sectors?

A common pattern is submitting a generic CV and cover letter to every employer, without adapting the content to reflect research into the specific sector, company or role — which recruiters in high-volume hiring environments notice quickly.

Q9

How do I build project evidence for a sector I have no work experience in?

Structured courses with practical assignments — such as a GIS mapping project, a digital marketing campaign plan, or a documented HSE risk assessment — can give you relevant, demonstrable project evidence even without prior sector-specific work experience.

Q10

Does having a digital skills certificate from a training provider carry weight with employers in these sectors?

It can be one useful piece of evidence among several, showing initiative and foundational knowledge, but it generally carries more weight when paired with a real project you completed and can discuss confidently, rather than the certificate alone.

Q11

Which sector — oil and gas, banking, or telecoms — is easiest to break into for a fresh graduate?

There is no reliable, honest answer to this, as competitiveness varies by year, specific employer, role type and the candidate's own background and preparation. It is more useful to focus on researching specific employers and roles within your sector of genuine interest than to try to identify an "easier" sector in general terms.
