How to Build a Graduate CV in Nigeria

A structure that gets past the first screen, even with little formal work history — and how to phrase a digital skill or capstone project so it actually counts.

Overview

A Nigerian graduate CV with little formal work history isn't a weak CV by default — it's a CV that hasn't yet been structured around what you actually have: NYSC experience, CDS projects, coursework capstones, and any self-directed practice. This guide covers how to structure that content so it gets past the first screen, as part of the full job-search roadmap.

If you haven't picked a skill worth putting on your CV yet, start there first — this guide assumes you have something specific to describe.

Key takeaways

  • NYSC PPA duties, CDS projects, coursework capstones, and personal projects are all legitimate CV content when framed around what you actually did.
  • One page is the safer default for entry-level applications in Nigeria.
  • A specific, outcome-focused bullet beats a vague "completed training" line every time.
  • A professional CV writer isn't required — a clear, honest, skills-forward structure covers most entry-level applications.
  • Whether to include a photo varies by sector; leaving it off is the safer default when unsure.

Structure

A CV structure that works with little formal experience

01

A short, specific summary

Two or three lines naming your actual skill and target industry — not a generic "hardworking team player seeking opportunities" statement.

02

A skills section naming specific capabilities

Name the actual skill (e.g. "GIS mapping using QGIS," "campaign analytics") rather than generic software familiarity as if it were a differentiator.

03

Project and experience section, outcome-first

NYSC PPA duties, CDS projects, and course capstones belong here, each described by what you did and what it demonstrates, not just that you attended.

04

Education, kept brief

Degree, institution, and graduation year — this section doesn't need to carry the weight of the whole CV once the sections above exist.

Practical examples

Phrasing a capstone project as a CV bullet

The difference is specificity, not length. A vague version: "Completed Digital Marketing training with Paleon Training." A stronger version, naming the actual outcome: "Built and documented a 4-week digital marketing campaign plan for a real brand scenario as a capstone project, covering channel strategy, content calendar, and basic analytics reporting."

The second version tells a recruiter what you can actually do, not just that you sat through a course. The same principle applies whether the project came from a Paleon capstone, a CDS assignment, or something you built independently.

Before you submit

Common CV mistakes

  • A generic objective statement that could apply to any candidate for any role, instead of naming your specific skill and target industry.
  • Unexplained gaps left blank rather than briefly accounted for — NYSC, further study, or skill-building periods are normal and don't need to be hidden.
  • Listing "Microsoft Word" or "Microsoft Excel" as a standout skill — baseline computer literacy isn't a differentiator on its own; name the specific, less common capability instead.
  • Describing a project by attendance, not contribution — "participated in a CDS project" is weaker than naming the specific thing you did within it.
  • Sending an identical CV to every employer regardless of industry — see the sector-specific guides for how to adapt it by industry.

Common questions

Frequently asked

Q1

What do I put on my CV if I have no work experience?

NYSC PPA duties, CDS projects, coursework capstones, and personal or portfolio projects are all legitimate CV content when framed around what you actually did and what skill it demonstrates.

Q2

Should a Nigerian graduate CV be one page or two?

One page is the safer default for entry-level applications. Two pages is acceptable once you have several distinct roles or projects worth including.

Q3

Do I need a professional CV writer?

Not necessarily — a clear, honest, skills-forward CV following a standard structure covers most entry-level applications. A paid CV service is a matter of budget and preference, not a requirement.

Q4

How do I list a Paleon course or capstone on my CV?

Name the course, the specific capstone project with a one-line outcome (not a vague "completed training"), and link to the project or certificate if you have an online version.

Q5

Should I include a photo on a Nigerian CV?

Practice varies by employer and sector. When unsure, a clean CV without a photo is the safer default for most private-sector digital roles.
