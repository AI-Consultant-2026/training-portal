## A Short History

The first computer virus appeared in the early 1970s — a program called **Creeper**. It wasn't malicious. It just displayed the message "I'm the creeper, catch me if you can" on infected computers — largely harmless, almost playful. But it proved something we're still dealing with fifty years later: if a computer can run a program, it can run a program you didn't want it to run.

In 1988, the **Morris Worm** accidentally took down about ten percent of the internet that existed at the time. That single incident led directly to the creation of the first Computer Emergency Response Teams — the kind of body ngCERT is here in Nigeria today.

By the 2000s, cybercrime had become organized — criminal groups and nation-states, not just curious teenagers. And here's the part that matters most for you: attackers don't only target huge multinational companies. **Small businesses, local government offices, schools, and hospitals** are targets too — often because they have weaker defenses.

## Four Threat Categories

### Malware

Malware means "malicious software" — **any program designed to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to a system.** Under that umbrella: a **virus** attaches to legitimate files and spreads when they're shared. A **worm** spreads on its own across networks, with no human needing to click anything. A **trojan** disguises itself as legitimate software — a "free antivirus" download that's actually spying on you. **Spyware** quietly monitors you and sends the data back to an attacker.

Malware always needs a way in. That's called an **attack vector** — an infected USB drive, a malicious email attachment, outdated software with a known flaw.

### Ransomware

Ransomware encrypts — locks — every file on a victim's system, then demands payment, usually in cryptocurrency, for the decryption key. It's the most financially damaging attack type of the last decade. Paying doesn't guarantee you get your files back. The prevention is almost always the same: **good backup practices and basic security hygiene.**

### Phishing

Phishing is a message — email, text, WhatsApp — pretending to be from someone trustworthy, designed to trick you into clicking a malicious link or typing your password into a fake page. What makes it effective isn't clever technology. It's **psychology** — urgency, fear, curiosity.

### Social Engineering

Social engineering is the umbrella term: **any attack that manipulates human psychology rather than exploiting a technical flaw.** Phishing is one form of it. **Pretexting** is another — an attacker invents a believable scenario, like posing as IT support to "verify your password." **Baiting** is a third — leaving an infected USB drive somewhere it's likely to be found and plugged in out of curiosity.

Here's the uncomfortable truth: the most expensive security software in the world can be undone by one employee clicking the wrong link. People are as much a part of the security picture as technology.
