# Lesson 1: Influencer Identification, Vetting & Partnerships

Welcome to our final week of Social Media Management and Content. This lesson covers influencer marketing — identifying the right partners, vetting them properly, and negotiating a partnership that actually protects both sides.

## What Influencer Marketing Actually Is

Influencer marketing means partnering with individuals who have built genuine, engaged audiences to promote a business, its products, or its services. This can range from major celebrities with millions of followers all the way down to **micro-influencers** — typically defined as having somewhere between roughly one thousand and one hundred thousand followers — who very often deliver dramatically better engagement rates and, importantly, far more affordable costs for smaller local businesses.

## Identifying the Right Influencer

Identifying the right influencer starts with **relevance**: does this person's actual audience genuinely overlap with your target personas from Week 2. It continues with **engagement quality**: a smaller account with consistently high, genuine engagement is very often more valuable than a much larger account with low, unresponsive engagement, which can sometimes even indicate purchased or fake followers.

## Vetting Before You Partner

**Vetting** means doing real, careful due diligence before any partnership: reviewing past content for consistent quality and genuine authenticity, checking for any previous controversies that could create meaningful brand risk, and honestly assessing whether their existing personal content style and values genuinely align with your brand's own positioning.

## Negotiating the Partnership

Once you've identified strong potential partners, formal negotiation begins. Key elements to clearly agree on include deliverables — precisely how many posts, of what type, on which specific platforms; compensation — which might be direct payment, free products or services, or some combination of both; usage rights — can the business legally repost or repurpose the influencer's created content elsewhere, and for how long; and, importantly, disclosure requirements — in most jurisdictions, including Nigeria, paid partnerships legally must be clearly, honestly disclosed to the audience, typically through a specific hashtag or platform-provided partnership label.

A written contract, even a genuinely simple one, protects both parties by clearly documenting all of these specific terms and timelines, and I'd strongly encourage you to never rely purely on an informal, verbal agreement for any paid partnership.

## Content Guidelines Without Killing Authenticity

While influencer content should always feel authentic and genuinely in the creator's own voice, not scripted like a traditional advertisement, providing clear brand guidelines still helps ensure consistent quality and messaging: key talking points that genuinely must be included, specific things to clearly avoid saying, and any required visual brand elements. The best influencer partnerships strike a careful, deliberate balance — enough guidance to protect the brand's core messaging, while still leaving genuine creative freedom for the authentic voice that made that influencer's audience trust and engage with them in the first place.

## Bringing It Together

This lesson covered the full arc of building an influencer partnership: identifying the right person based on relevance and engagement quality, vetting them properly before committing, negotiating clear deliverables, compensation, usage rights, and disclosure, and providing guidelines that protect the brand without stripping away the authenticity that made the partnership worth pursuing in the first place.
