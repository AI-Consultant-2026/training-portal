## A Short History

**Facebook** launched in **2004**, originally for university students. **Twitter** followed in 2006, built around short, real-time updates. **Instagram** arrived in 2010, centered entirely on visual content. **TikTok** launched internationally in **2018**, and it changed the industry by proving that **short-form video, driven by an algorithm rather than who you follow, could dominate people's attention.**

Platforms rise and decline, but the underlying skills stay constant: **understanding an audience, telling a compelling story, and building genuine engagement.** That's what this course focuses on.

[CUT TO: six-platform comparison graphic]

## Platform Comparison

Treating every platform the same is one of the most common beginner mistakes. Each one has a different purpose.

**Facebook** remains enormous for reaching an **older demographic and local community engagement** — local business pages, community groups, marketplace activity.

**Instagram** is built around **visual storytelling** — photos, Reels, and 24-hour Stories. Strong for food, fashion, tourism, beauty.

**TikTok** is built entirely around short-form video and a **powerful discovery algorithm** that can surface your content to people who've never heard of your brand — but it demands a fast-paced, authentic style.

**LinkedIn** is the professional network — valuable for **B2B businesses, professional services, and recruitment**, with a more informative, industry-focused content style.

**Twitter/X** is built around **real-time conversation, news, and public commentary** — useful for customer service and thought leadership.

**YouTube** is the platform for long-form video and, notably, the **second-largest search engine in the world** — content gets discovered through search long after it was posted.

Your first strategic decision is never "how do we post more." It's **which platforms actually match where your target audience spends their time.** Being excellent on two platforms beats being mediocre on all six.

## Goals and KPIs

Before you post a single piece of content, you need clarity on one question: what is this presence actually supposed to achieve?

Common goals: **brand awareness** — more people knowing the business exists; **engagement** — an interactive community; **lead generation** — driving people toward a purchase; **customer service** — using social as a support channel.

Each goal needs matching **Key Performance Indicators (KPIs).** Brand awareness → **reach and impressions.** Engagement → **likes, comments, shares, and saves.** Lead generation → click-through rate and conversions.

Here's what to internalize now: **vanity metrics — like a large follower count — feel good but don't always reflect real business value.** A page with **50,000 followers and almost no engagement** is often worth less than a page with **2,000 highly engaged followers who actually buy regularly.** Choosing KPIs that genuinely match your goal is what keeps your work grounded in real results, not appearances.
